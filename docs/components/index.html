IRO components and contracts

  • Author: Itay Etelis
  • Date: 2026-10-12

Problem​

Recovery involves three parties: the controller, the workload that runs the Pods, and the inference engine. If they share the details of one engine or one workload type, such as rank numbers, store addresses, status strings or ports, then every new engine, every new workload type, and scaling back up after a shrink means changing all three. This doc fixes the boundaries: the controller sees only Pods and members, and everything specific to an engine or a workload stays behind its plugin.

Traffic router (EPP)routes on the engine's native health. IRO never steers traffic.

Example group​

The examples in these pages use one group: four Pods, pod-0 to pod-3 (UIDs a1 to d1, identities idA to idD), with one member each, m0 to m3. See them step by step in Failures and Examples.

One reconciliation step​

Two kinds of action, with opposite delivery rules:

ActionDeliveryWhy
Engine reconfig (Apply)at most one run. Saved first. Sent again, same ID, only when the send was never confirmed and Progress proves no member got it.running a reconfig twice can break the group, so a resend needs proof, and a member that already got it ignores it
Workload call (Reset, ResetAll, Release)saved first, repeated every poll until Discover shows the resulteach call names Pods by UID and does nothing once they are gone, so repeating it is harmless

Open decisions​

  1. Disaggregated sets. A prefill/decode set has several engine groups with different roles. Proposal: one recovery unit per engine group, each with its own Group, journal and controller loop.
  2. Pods with some members excluded. With several members per Pod, losing one member does not let IRO reset the Pod (C8), so that capacity stays lost until ResetAll. Should the policy be allowed to remove the healthy rest of the Pod so the Pod can be reset?
