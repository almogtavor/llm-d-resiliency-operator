LWS workload

How LeaderWorkerSet implements the workload and journal contracts. Code: internal/workload/lws and internal/journal/configmap.

Workload​

ContractLWS
Groupone LWS with replicas: 1, Pods with label group-index=0
Required specsize >= 1, groupIdentity: Ordinal or unset, restartPolicy unset, RecreateGroupOnPodRestart or None
Pod.Indexlabel leaderworkerset.sigs.k8s.io/worker-index. Names are <lws>-0 and <lws>-0-<i>.
Pod.Identitya hash of the Pod UID and, per engine container, its ID and restart count. Engine containers are --lws-engine-containers, by default every container in spec.containers. Init containers, native sidecars included, count only when named. Changing the flag changes every identity, which IRO reads as a new group.
Pod.Nodespec.nodeName
Pod.StateTerminating when the Pod is being deleted. Ready when every container runs and the Pod has an IP. Otherwise Starting.
Recreatingthe leader Pod is missing or terminating, carries leaderworkerset.sigs.k8s.io/group-recreate-pending, or is newer than a worker (LWS replaced it while old workers remain). Under RecreateGroupOnPodRestart, also any Pod terminating or with a restarted or exited container.
RecreateStoppedthe leader Pod carries leaderworkerset.sigs.k8s.io/group-restart-budget-exhausted
Discover failsthe LWS cannot be read, its spec is not supported, or a Pod's name does not match its worker index
Resetunder RecreateGroupOnPodRestart: ErrUnsupported, because deleting any Pod recreates the whole group. Under None: delete each named worker with a UID precondition, and LWS recreates each one alone. A list that names the leader: ErrUnsupported before anything is deleted, because deleting it takes its workers with it.
ResetAlldelete the leader with a UID precondition and foreground propagation, so the old leader stays Terminating until its workers are gone. NotFound or Conflict count as done.
ReleaseErrUnsupported: LWS cannot remove a chosen Pod from a running group
Flags--lws-namespace (default default), --lws-name, --lws-engine-containers (each Pod must have at least one of them, or Discover fails; leader and workers may name theirs differently)

Journal​

ContractConfigMap
StorageConfigMap <lws-name>-ft-state, key state.json
Version<resourceVersion>/<digest of state.json>
Compare-and-swapread the ConfigMap, and update it only if its resourceVersion or its state.json matches the version. Anything else, including a Kubernetes conflict, is ErrConflict.
Metadata-only changestate.json is unchanged, so the update goes ahead on the new resourceVersion
DeletedErrConflict. The empty version creates the ConfigMap, and AlreadyExists is ErrConflict.
Lifetimeowner reference to the LWS

RBAC​

leaderworkersets get; pods list and delete; configmaps get, create and update; leases for --leader-elect; events create and patch. See deploy/kubernetes/rbac.yaml.

Open decisions​

  1. Restart policy. Resetting one Pod needs restartPolicy: None. Under RecreateGroupOnPodRestart, Reset answers ErrUnsupported, and any container restart, including one from a probe, recreates the group: IRO waits and adopts it. Under None, LWS replaces only a deleted Pod, and kubelet restarts a crashed container in place. Either way IRO removes the lost member in place. A deleted Pod's replacement brings members that stay candidates until the engine can admit them (vLLM cannot), holding their GPU. An engine container restarted in place keeps the Pod's UID: its new members are never candidates, and the Pod is reset like any failed Pod (with vLLM, kept as it is). Neither policy tolerates a probe that fails while a member is paused for recovery: see the engine's doc.
  2. Restart limits for IRO resets. restartBackoff and maxGroupRestarts are on LWS main, not in v0.11.1. Check that a recreation IRO causes, by deleting the leader, is paced and counted like any other.
